Public-secret framing

The story is not one secret. It is a stack of public records.

Arizona history is best read as overlapping systems: land, water, minerals, roads, rail, military testing, border policy, tribal sovereignty, federal land management, and metro growth. The “secret” layer is usually not classified material; it is the pattern that appears when county records, agency reports, declassified memos, procurement notices, maps, legislative hearings, and archives are read together.

GovINT treats every dramatic claim as a source-chain problem: who said it, what record supports it, where is the primary file, what does the map show, and what reliability label should attach?

Primary-source paths

  • Arizona State Library, Archives and Public Records for territorial and state material.
  • County recorder and assessor systems for property, maps, and development history.
  • Arizona Legislature for statutes, committee minutes, bills, and budget history.
  • National Archives and presidential libraries for federal history and declassified context.
  • GAO, CRS, Federal Register, and GovInfo for policy reports and federal notices.
  • USGS, Bureau of Reclamation, ADWR, CAP, SRP, and ADEQ for water, geology, and infrastructure.

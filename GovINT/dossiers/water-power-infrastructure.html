Arizona is governed through water and power.

Arizona's public history cannot be separated from infrastructure. Land speculation, agriculture, mining, nuclear power, city growth, semiconductor plants, golf-course politics, tribal water settlements, and drought all connect to public records. This dossier organizes those records into a readable civic intelligence layer.

Water record stack

  • Arizona Department of Water Resources: groundwater, assured water supply, basin planning, conservation, and maps.
  • Central Arizona Project: Colorado River water delivery, canal infrastructure, board documents, and planning reports.
  • Bureau of Reclamation Lower Colorado Region: river operations, shortage declarations, dams, and federal planning.
  • County flood-control districts: washes, drainage, hazard mapping, and capital improvement programs.
  • Tribal water settlements: public settlement documents, congressional materials, and implementation reports.
  • Municipal water departments: drought plans, rate cases, conservation programs, and infrastructure bonds.

Power record stack

  • Arizona Corporation Commission dockets for utility regulation and rate cases.
  • Palo Verde public filings, NRC materials, emergency planning pages, and regional power context.
  • SRP public resources for water, power, watershed, and grid programs.
  • APS and utility integrated resource planning documents.
  • EIA, DOE, and FERC public datasets for generation and grid context.
  • City and county meeting packets for zoning, substations, transmission, and energy facilities.

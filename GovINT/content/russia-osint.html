Russia OSINT — Sources, Method & Scope
How the Russia / Eurasia module is built, what it includes, and where the line is drawn
METHODOLOGY DOSSIER

Purpose

The Russia / Eurasia module extends GovINT's public-data approach beyond Arizona to open-source situational awareness for the Eurasian theatre. It aggregates publicly available, mostly official data into a browsable, cross-referenced research surface with reliability labels on every claim. It is an educational and research tool — not a news outlet, not an intelligence service, and not an operational targeting system.

What it covers

  • Cyber threat actors — Russian state-linked APT groups (APT28/29, Sandworm, Turla, Gamaredon, Star Blizzard) drawn from MITRE ATT&CK and CISA/NSA advisories, plus Russia-relevant advisories filtered from live feeds.
  • Official .ru sources — a directory of executive, legislative, ministry, statistics, financial, registry, transport, energy, regulatory, and cyber bodies.
  • Sanctions & ownership — live OFAC SDN designations (Russia/Ukraine programs), strategic industries and "whale companies", critical export commodities, and jurisdiction-level evasion typologies.
  • Civilian & economic context — population hubs, federal districts, macro economy, energy/commodity production, metro systems, and manufacturing geography.
  • History — a Cold War-to-present timeline including international conflicts, and decommissioned Cold War heritage museums.
  • Environment — crowdsourced air-quality sensor readings.

How data flows

Collectors in scripts/ run daily via GitHub Actions and write JSON into data/latest/:

  • fetch_russia_cyber.py → russia_apt.json — CISA/NSA advisories filtered for Russia relevance, merged with a curated MITRE actor set.
  • fetch_russia_news.py → russia_news.json — mixed-reliability English RSS (state media labeled).
  • fetch_russia_sensors.py → russia_sensors.json — keyless Sensor.Community air quality.
  • fetch_russia_sanctions.py → russia_sanctions.json — official OFAC SDN designations; personal free-text remarks stripped.

Curated reference data lives in data/wiki/russia.json; the dashboards are russia.html, russia-networks.html, and russia-history.html.

Source reliability

Every item carries a reliability label. Crucially, Russian state and state-media sources are labeled media claim so they are never presented as verified fact — they reflect official narratives and must be cross-referenced against independent reporting and primary records. Official designations (OFAC, CISA) are official; aggregated third-party datasets (OpenSanctions, OCCRP, FAS, SIPRI) are archived.

Scope & boundaries

This module is deliberately limited to aggregate, structural, and historical public data. It does not, and will not:

  • Geolocate active military or nuclear-weapons facilities, command bunkers, or storage sites.
  • Map military recruitment offices or other facilities as potential physical targets.
  • Track the real-time physical movements of named individuals (jets, yachts, residences, family).

Those uses cross from open-source research into targeting, and they are out of scope regardless of intent. Where the underlying interest is legitimate — e.g., understanding nuclear posture — the module points to the open arms-control scholarship (FAS Nuclear Notebook, Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists, SIPRI, Pavel Podvig) that publishes treaty-level analysis responsibly, rather than reproducing facility-level detail.

Legal & ethical note

All sources are publicly broadcast or officially published. Some providers (e.g., sanctions aggregators, sensor networks) have terms of service governing reuse; consult each provider before bulk redistribution. The goal is transparency and situational awareness for education and research — not law enforcement, not surveillance, not targeting.

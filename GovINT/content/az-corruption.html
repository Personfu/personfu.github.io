Overview

Arizona public corruption cases involve elected officials, law enforcement, local government administrators, and state agency employees. The FBI Phoenix Field Office and the U.S. Attorney's Office for the District of Arizona handle federal prosecutions under 18 U.S.C. §201 (bribery), §666 (federal program fraud), §1341-1343 (mail/wire fraud), and RICO statutes where applicable. The Arizona Attorney General's Office handles state-level prosecutions.

This tracker aggregates publicly documented cases from DOJ press releases, court records, and investigative reporting. All information is sourced from public records.

Major Cases — Federal Prosecutions

Operation Three Card Monte — Tucson (2015)
DOJ District of Arizona · Multiple defendants · Guilty pleas obtained
FBI Tucson undercover operation targeting Pima County and City of South Tucson officials. Multiple defendants accepted bribes in exchange for favorable government contracts and zoning decisions. Included a city council member, planning commissioners, and private contractors. FBI agents posed as business interests seeking permits and contracts. Charges: bribery of local officials, conspiracy, honest services fraud.

Source: DOJ USAO Arizona press releases

Arizona AZSCAM — 1991
Arizona AG investigation · State-level sting · 7 legislators convicted
The Arizona Organized Crime and Corruption Act sting (AZSCAM) was an Arizona DPS undercover operation in which a paid informant posed as a casino lobbyist seeking to legalize casino gambling. The sting captured 7 state legislators on video accepting cash bribes in exchange for votes — in a state where gambling was not yet legal. One-third of the targeted legislators were indicted. The operation remains one of the largest state-level corruption stings in U.S. history. The resulting scandal contributed to the passage of Indian gaming legislation and exposed the mechanics of legislative corruption.

Source: Arizona Department of Public Safety historical records

Maricopa County Sheriff Joe Arpaio — Federal Contempt (2017)
U.S. District Court, District of Arizona · Convicted · Pardoned by President Trump
Maricopa County Sheriff Joe Arpaio (serving 1993-2016) was convicted in July 2017 of criminal contempt of court for defying a 2011 federal court order to stop racially profiling Latino drivers and detaining people based solely on suspected immigration status. A federal judge found Arpaio had willfully violated the injunction for 18 months. President Trump pardoned Arpaio in August 2017 before sentencing. The case involved documented systemic violations of the constitutional rights of Latino residents of Maricopa County over a period of years. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit upheld the contempt conviction itself, ruling the pardon did not erase it from the record.

Source: DOJ press release; District of Arizona court records

Arizona Sting — Dark Money / Campaign Finance (2022)
FBI Phoenix · Active investigation pattern · Multiple referrals
Campaign finance enforcement in Arizona has focused on "dark money" — nonprofit organizations that spend on political campaigns without disclosing donors. The Citizens Clean Elections Commission and Arizona Attorney General's office have pursued enforcement actions against dark money groups that violated disclosure requirements. The U.S. Supreme Court's 2021 Brnovich v. DNC decision, arising from Arizona election law challenges, set precedent affecting dark money disclosure nationally. Several investigations into coordination between dark money groups and campaigns are ongoing per DOJ public records.

Source: Arizona Citizens Clean Elections Commission; FEC enforcement actions

Cochise County Board of Supervisors — Election Certification Refusal (2022)
Arizona AG / DOJ Elections Section · Court action · Resolved
In November 2022, the Cochise County Board of Supervisors — a rural county in southeastern Arizona (seat: Bisbee) — initially refused to certify the results of the 2022 midterm election, claiming unspecified concerns about voting machine certification. The Arizona Supreme Court ultimately ordered the county to certify. Two supervisors who voted to delay faced Arizona AG investigation and the state Supreme Court found them to have acted unlawfully. The incident was part of a broader pattern of election certification disputes in Arizona following the 2020 election. Federal DOJ Election Section monitored the situation.

Source: Arizona Supreme Court records; Arizona AG press releases

Yuma — Human Smuggling Network (Ongoing 2020s)
DOJ / FBI Phoenix · Multiple indictments · Ongoing
The Yuma border corridor has been the site of ongoing FBI and HSI investigations into human smuggling networks, some of which have involved corruption of local law enforcement and government employees. Federal indictments have named individuals working for organizations that paid bribes to border area law enforcement for intelligence about checkpoint operations and patrol routes. These cases are prosecuted under federal human trafficking and bribery statutes. Specific case details available via DOJ USAO-AZ press releases.

Source: DOJ U.S. Attorney's Office, District of Arizona press releases
